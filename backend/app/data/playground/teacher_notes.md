# AI Teacher notes

Teaching notes for the AI Teacher playground. Every answer the bot gives is
grounded in this file: it retrieves the passages closest to what the child is
learning today and explains from those, rather than from whatever the model
happens to remember.

Written for a child of two to six, sitting with a grown-up. Every line is
English. Where a Hindi letter is taught, the Devanagari is quoted inside the
English sentence and never transliterated into Roman letters.

---

## Numbers: one, two, three

The number 1 is one thing on its own. One nose. One moon in the sky. When we
count, 1 is always the first word we say.

The number 2 is a pair - two things that go together. Two eyes, two hands, two
shoes. If something comes in a pair, it is 2.

The number 3 is one more than two. Three wheels on a tricycle. To count three
things, touch each one and say one, two, three. The last word you say is how
many there are.

Counting rule: touch each thing only once, and say the numbers in order. The
last number you say is the answer.

The written shape of 3 has two little curves, one on top of the other, like two
tiny hills stacked up. It opens to the left.

## Numbers: four, five

The number 4 is one more than three. A table has four legs. A car has four
wheels. Four is two pairs put together.

The number 5 is how many fingers are on one hand. Hold up your hand: that is 5.
Five toes on one foot too.

To check five things, count them in a line, or split them into a pair and a
group of three.

## Numbers: six to ten

Six is five and one more, so it is one whole hand and one finger. Seven is five
and two. Eight is a pair of four - eight legs on a spider. Nine is one less than
ten. Ten is both hands, all the fingers together.

When a number is hard, go back to five: count to five first, then keep going.

## Letters: A, B, C

The letter A says "aa", like in apple. Big A looks like a tent with a belt
across the middle. Small a is a little circle with a stick behind it.

The letter B says "buh", like in ball and in banana. Big B is a straight line
with two tummies on the right side.

The letter C says "kuh", like in cat and in cup. C is an open curve, like a
mouth about to eat, or the moon when it is not full.

Rule for letters: every letter has a name and a sound. A is the name; "aa" is
the sound it makes inside a word.

## Letters: D, E, F

D says "duh", like in dog and door. Big D is a straight line with one big tummy.

E says "eh", like in egg and elephant. Big E is a line with three arms going
right - top, middle, bottom.

F says "fuh", like in fish and fan. F is E with the bottom arm taken away.

## Letters: writing them

To write A: draw a slide down to the left, a slide down to the right, then a
little belt across the middle.

To write B: one line straight down, then a tummy at the top and a tummy at the
bottom.

To write C: start at the top, curve around to the left, and stop before you
close it.

Say the movement out loud while the child draws. Never say whether the drawing
is good enough - the app measures the shape, the teacher only says where to
start and which way to go.

## Shapes

A circle is round all the way with no corners at all. A wheel, a plate, the sun.
Trace it with your finger and you never stop.

A square has four sides that are all the same length, and four corners. A window,
a biscuit, a slice of bread.

A triangle has three straight sides and three corners. A slice of pizza, a roof,
a party hat.

A rectangle has four corners like a square, but two sides are long and two are
short. A door, a book, a phone.

Rule for shapes: count the corners. No corners is a circle, three is a triangle,
four is a square or a rectangle.

## Colours

Red is the colour of a tomato, a fire engine, and a ripe apple.

Blue is the colour of the sky on a clear day and of deep water.

Yellow is the colour of the sun, a banana, and a lemon.

Green is the colour of leaves and grass.

To help a child find a colour, name two things in the room that are that colour
before asking them to point at it.

## Hindi letters

The letter अ is the first letter of the Hindi alphabet. It is taught with the
word अनार, which means pomegranate: "अ से अनार".

The letter आ comes next and is अ with a standing line added. It is taught with
आम, which means mango.

The letter क says a sound like "kuh". It is taught with कमल, which means lotus.

Rule for Hindi: say the letter, then the word it starts, then what that word
means in English. Never write the Hindi word in Roman letters.

## How to teach one thing

Say what it is in one short sentence. Point at something the child can actually
see. Ask one small question about it. Wait. If the answer is wrong, do not
repeat the same sentence louder - change how you are showing it: a story, a
different example, doing it together, or fewer choices.

A child who gets it wrong has not failed. It means the explanation has not
landed yet, and it is the explanation that has to change.

## Words that work with small children

Short sentences. One idea at a time. Things they can see and touch. Warmth
before correction: "good trying" comes before "let us look again". Never the
words wrong, bad, failed, or no. Never more than one question in a turn.
